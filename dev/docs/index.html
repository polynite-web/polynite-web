A burst of colour

Launch, freeze the action, orbit and let it tumble.

Open scene ↗
Try the prompt

Create a compact 4 × 4 × 4 cluster of colourful physical blocks. Hold for about 1.16 seconds, then launch the blocks outward and upward. Add a bold orange EXPLODE title in 3D with a slight tilt and a small rotation during its typewriter entrance, then slide it through a fixed left-edge mask while it fades in opacity. Launch the blocks three quarters of the way through its exit, with no hold on the complete word. After the blocks spread, freeze the physics for 1.8 seconds while the camera orbits 55 degrees, then resume the physics. Finish with the Polynite signature over the scene dimmed by 75%, beginning the dimming 0.2 seconds before the logo and keeping the camera orbit moving throughout the closing signature.

Connect your assistant to recreate it, then change the colours, scale or camera.

Get started

  1. Open Polynite in a desktop browser with WebGPU. Use procedural scene controls, or connect a compatible assistant using the MCP integration.
  2. Start small: create a few objects, inspect them and keep editing. Positions use metres; blocks of 0.25–0.4 m are a useful starting point.
  3. For conversational creation, ask in the connected assistant. Follow-ups target the same scene; a lost connection should be recovered before further edits.

No imported GLB is required to begin. Explore Polynite on YouTube ↗

Video signatures

The tutorial films include a short opening cue and a Polynite logo with a closing sound. The updated native engine offers independent Intro, Outro and Watermark controls in the cinematic player. Web controls require the matching engine update.

For compatible engines, the MCP can use local scene.cinematic with request.command set_branding, intro polynite-spark or none, outro polynite-glow or none, volume 0..2 and optional boolean watermark. Check scene.capabilities.cinematic.branding before requesting this command.

Cloud video links and retention

Download films you want to keep. Uploaded MCP videos have seven-day retention; signed read links expire within one hour and can be refreshed while the file remains available. Anyone with a signed link can view the video until expiry.

Current limits

  • Rendering requires WebGPU and a ready viewer for scene edits.
  • Cloud uploads: 32 MiB/video, 16 videos/scene, 12 new uploads/account/day.
  • Recording accepts 30/60 FPS and 720/1080p. Encoder availability/formats depend on the browser.
  • MCP checkpoints retain data for seven days. Missing model files may need to be supplied again.
  • Long Firefox sessions have an unresolved memory-crash report. Firefox endurance and mobile/Safari recording are not certified.
  • Complex articulated scenes require verification. Command acceptance does not prove a correct visible result.

See troubleshooting.

Models, providers and access

Importing and inspecting a local model is separate from sharing or using an AI service. Sharing, checkpoints and cloud video delivery can upload data; connected generation providers may require an account and charge for usage. See data handling.

Jev connections inside the app, Polynite credits and local AI are foundations in development, not public features of the current MCP integration. The plugin has not yet been approved for the public OpenAI directory.
