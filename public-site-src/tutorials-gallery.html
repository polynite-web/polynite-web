Advanced

Brick wall lab

Tune the impact, dust and render budget with 17 scene-owned controls.

Open scene ↗
Try it step by step

Create a procedural brick wall and a wrecking ball on a physical chain. Add custom controls for impact, masses, wall height, dust and render quality, with Gentle, Breach, Demolition, Mobile and Cinematic presets.

Connect your assistant to recreate it, then make it your own.

  1. Open Scene options (⋯), then Scene settings. The downloaded scene contains 17 controls and five presets.
  2. Start with Breach. On a recent viewer, edits stay pending and Play turns gold: choose Apply to reset paused, or Apply & Play / Replay to run the new values. Discard keeps the current scene unchanged.
  3. Compare Gentle and Demolition. Keep the same camera and watch how mass and impact speed change the opening and the scattered bricks.
  4. Enable impact dust. Try amount 0.85, cloud size 0.28 m, lifetime 2.4 s, opacity 0.65 and minimum impact energy 1 J.
  5. Choose Mobile for 18 wall rows, lighter shading/LOD and a smaller dust budget. Compare it with Cinematic; do not enable motion blur for the mobile test.
  6. On a recent viewer, hold a brick or the ball while the film plays, then drag and release. The hold wheel is larger and offset on touch screens.
  7. Use the camera toggle to explore freely. Pause leaves the camera movable; scene-camera playback adds its movement to your own framing. The speed slider changes preview speed without changing the MP4.
  8. Save scene preserves custom settings, procedural recipes and the complete film sequence. Record MP4 from the initial state; the ending signature appears in the recorded video, not the live preview.
Custom settings · 17 controls

These settings belong to this scene and are saved with it.

Impact and structure

Impact speed 1–15 m/s
Initial push in metres per second. Increase speed before reducing brick mass.
Ball mass 10–100 kg
A heavier ball carries more momentum at the same speed.
Brick mass 0.5–10 kg
Heavier bricks resist the impact; lighter bricks scatter more easily.
Brick friction 0.1–1.5 coefficient
Higher friction reduces sliding after the impact.
Release angle 5–55 deg
Starting angle of the suspended ball, measured in degrees.
Wall rows 5–30
Generate a shorter or taller wall without placing each brick manually.
Show chain On / Off
Show or hide the link meshes; the suspension physics remains active.

Impact dust

Impact dust On / Off
Enable the bounded collision-particle effect.
Dust amount 0–1
More visible dust without creating more physical bodies.
Cloud size 0.02–0.75 m
Size of the individual dust puffs, in metres.
Cloud lifetime 0.2–4 s
How long each puff remains visible.
Dust opacity 0–1
Control transparency separately from cloud size and lifetime. Updated viewer required.
Minimum impact energy 0–100 J
Only collisions above this energy emit dust. Lower it for gentler impacts. Updated viewer required.

Surface and performance

Render quality Mobile / Balanced / Export
Mobile uses lighter geometry and effects; Balanced is the everyday profile; Export uses full meshes.
Motion blur 0–1
Rigid-object motion blur. It is disabled by the Mobile profile.
Brick edge radius 0–0.008 m
Small bevels catch light along the edges. This remains bounded for the physics shape.
Brick colour variation 0–0.3
Seeded colour variation shared by the procedural wall recipe.
Try 4 guided experiments
  1. 1. Gentle versus Demolition
    Try Gentle (50 kg ball, 6 kg bricks, 2 m/s), then Demolition (100 kg ball, 0.75 kg bricks, 12 m/s). Apply and replay each preset; compare the opening and the debris.
  2. 2. Make dust easier to see
    Use Breach, then increase cloud size to 0.4 m and lifetime to 3 s. Change only one value at a time. On an updated viewer, lower the energy threshold for lighter impacts.
  3. 3. Build for a phone
    Choose Mobile: 18 rows, no motion blur and bounded dust. Check responsiveness on a real phone. Visual LOD does not change the physical colliders.
  4. 4. Frame and record
    Choose Cinematic for a desktop video. Preview with a free camera or at 0.5x, then record the authored sequence as MP4. Save the .scene alongside the video to keep the controls.

Some controls need an updated viewer. Older viewers apply edits immediately and may omit dust opacity/energy threshold. Queued edits, live grabbing, camera modes and speed controls require a recent build.

MCP example

Select the Mobile preset in the current scene. An explicit MCP settings_preset applies immediately and resets paused; use settings_replay to run it. UI edits on a recent viewer remain pending until you choose Apply or Play.

Use polynite_scene with these arguments and a fresh action_id. Keep the current scene.

{
  "operation": "settings_preset",
  "action_id": "replace-with-a-fresh-uuid",
  "preset": "mobile"
}

MCP example

This snippet adds the illustrative command field only. The blue colour change is a separate action in the downloadable scene.

Use polynite_scene with operation: local, method: scene.cinematic, a new action_id, and this object as params.request. replace changes the whole timeline; keep existing steps when editing.

{
  "command": "replace",
  "seq": [
    {
      "op": "hold",
      "at": 0,
      "dur": 4
    },
    {
      "op": "text",
      "preset": "command-input-v2",
      "text": "Make it blue",
      "at": 0.4
    }
  ]
}
